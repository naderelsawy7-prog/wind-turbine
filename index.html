Regional screening values collected from the Drive feasibility report; not a substitute for site measurement.

The source report recommends at least 12 months of wind measurement at hub height for project-grade assessment. Secondary Egyptian sites are reported at roughly 20–25% capacity factor.

Data note: The source workbook labels its annual energy result “MW”; its formula is kW × 8,760 ÷ 1,000, so the unit is MWh/year. It also labels energy divided by a target as “capacity factor”; this calculator separates target coverage from actual/site capacity factor.
Beginner guide: what each number means

Quick unit rule: kW is the turbine’s power at a moment; MWh/year is the total electricity over a whole year. For wind speed, enter a typical average—not a storm gust.

First inputs: turbine & site

Turbine type
HAWT blades turn like a propeller. VAWT blades turn around an upright shaft.
Rated power (kW)
The maximum electrical output printed on the turbine’s nameplate. It is not what the turbine makes all the time.
Rotor diameter (m)
The width of the area swept by the blades; for a VAWT, enter the rotor’s width.
Rotor height (m)
The height of the rotating part of a VAWT. The calculator uses width × height as an approximate wind-catching area.
Average wind speed (m/s)
The usual wind speed at the turbine location and height. A 12-month measurement is much better than a short observation.
Site profile
A rough regional starting point for Egypt. It is not a measurement of your exact site.
Capacity factor (CF, %)
Average yearly output as a share of rated power. At 35%, a 1 kW turbine averages about 0.35 kW over the year.

Advanced: height & power

Hub height (m)
How high the rotor’s center sits above the ground. Wind can be stronger higher up.
Air density (kg/m³)
How much air is packed into a space. Denser air carries more wind energy; keep the standard value if you do not know yours.
Adjust wind to hub height
Turn this on only when your wind reading was taken at a different height from the turbine rotor.
Measurement height (m)
The height where the anemometer or other wind instrument measured the wind.
Shear exponent (α)
A setting for how quickly wind speed changes with height. It depends on the site; the default is only a rough assumption.
Power coefficient (Cp)
The share of wind power the blades capture. It varies by turbine and operating speed; 0.593 is a theoretical upper limit, not a normal default.
System efficiency (%)
The share of rotor power that reaches the electrical output after drivetrain and generator losses.
Cut-in speed (m/s)
The wind speed at which the turbine starts producing power in this estimate.
Cut-out speed (m/s)
An optional high-wind safety shutdown speed from the turbine datasheet. Leave blank if unknown.

Advanced: energy & spacing

Annual energy target (MWh/year)
The amount of electricity you hope to generate in a year. It is used for comparison only.
Turbine spacing (m)
The downwind distance from one turbine to the next. A turbine can slow the wind behind it.
Minimum spacing (× D)
A simple rule of thumb based on rotor diameter D. For example, 5D means five rotor diameters; it is not a final engineering layout rule.
Thrust coefficient (Ct)
A measure of how strongly the rotor slows the wind. It helps estimate the wake behind a turbine.
Wake decay (κ)
How quickly the slowed wind recovers farther behind the turbine. It depends on the site.
Wake formula
Standard Jensen is the recommended screening formula. Workbook compatibility is only for comparing with the older spreadsheet.

What the results mean

Swept area
The space the rotor passes through and takes wind from.
Power in wind
The wind’s theoretical power before the rotor captures any of it.
Rotor mechanical power
The estimated power the blades take from the wind, before electrical losses.
Estimated electrical output
A rough power estimate after efficiency and the rated-power cap. It is not a manufacturer power curve.
Equivalent rated speed
The speed this simplified model needs to reach rated power. It is not the turbine maker’s official rated wind speed.
Site-based annual energy (AEP)
AEP means annual energy production. Here it uses rated power × 8,760 hours × the selected capacity factor.
Target coverage
Site-based annual energy divided by your target. It tells how much of the target the estimate reaches.
Target CF required
The capacity factor needed to meet your target with the chosen rated power.
Flat-speed annualization
What the energy would be if the entered wind speed lasted every hour of the year. Real wind changes, so do not treat this as AEP.
Wake wind / point-power loss
A simplified estimate of wind speed and power behind another turbine. The loss is at that one wind speed—not an annual energy loss.
